# Robot graphics packs

The owner wants at least three graphics packs for Modular AI Robotics: **Low, Medium and High**. **Ultra is optional**, with a target of greater mechanical and surface detail resembling the supplied robot images. This replaces a universal assumption that every robot must use simplified block geometry.

The minimum pack offering and optional Ultra direction are owner-selected. The visual breakdown, default and delivery approach below are proposals for the rendering and asset work.

## Proposed quality tiers

| Pack | Geometry and surfaces | Decorative motion and effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Simple forms, economical textures, clear optics and a few strong mechanical details | Essential poses and status cues, restrained secondary motion and particles |
| Medium | Layered panels, recognizable joints, clearer materials and moderate wear | More cosmetic articulation and modest effects; proposed balanced default |
| High | Richer mechanical geometry, deeper optics, grippers, cables and finer surface detail | Fuller secondary motion and richer decorative effects within measured limits |
| Ultra optional | Dense mechanical detail, layered lens assemblies, hinges, hoses and detailed surface finishes approaching the references | The most elaborate presentation supported by the eventual renderer and measured performance budget |

High and Ultra may use more rounded and articulated shapes. Preserve each robot's proportions, palette and recognizable silhouette across all tiers, with simpler approximations in Low. Graphics quality should scale geometry, textures and effects together.

## Shared gameplay and information

Use the same robot identity, capabilities, inventory, collision shape, reach, interaction positions and authoritative job behavior in every pack. Keep equipment mounts and tool contact points aligned across visual variants. A graphics tier must not change which tasks a robot can perform.

Keep important job, equipment, cargo and status information readable in Low. Blue optics and amber indicators should remain useful without depending on elaborate lighting effects. Cosmetic motion can vary while action timing and essential status cues stay consistent.

The proposed preference is for players to choose their own visual quality. Pack packaging, model selection, renderer support and any optional material or lighting features remain technical decisions for later verification. The word pack here records the intended offering; it does not choose a specific Minecraft resource-pack mechanism.

## Asset development and performance

Start with one robot shown at all three required tiers, adding an Ultra study if useful. Use that comparison to establish a common visual language before building variants for every chassis. Share proportions, pose definitions and equipment placement, then vary decorative geometry and surfaces.

Set texture, geometry, animation and effect budgets from actual rendering measurements, including scenes with multiple active robots. Record frame time, memory use and visual readability before fixing numerical limits or hardware targets. Ultra is an optional detail target, not a measured performance claim.
